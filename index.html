About the Shed

Diesel Loco Shed, Sabarmati is a premier locomotive maintenance facility located at Sabarmati, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India. It falls under the Ahmedabad Railway Division of the Western Railway Zone of Indian Railways. The shed is situated approximately 6 kilometres east of Ahmedabad Junction on the Ahmedabad–Delhi main line, near the historic Sabarmati Ashram at Dharamnagar.

The shed caters to the maintenance, repair, and operational needs of both freight and passenger train locomotives. It is one of three diesel loco sheds in the Western Railway zone — the others being Ratlam and Vatva. It is also one of the last remaining major metre-gauge (MG) loco sheds in India.

The shed operates around the clock with dedicated engineering and operational staff ensuring the availability and reliability of locomotives for train operations across the region.

Facilities & Infrastructure

🔩 Loco Pits & Berthing Roads
Multiple inspection pits and berthing roads for simultaneous servicing and examination of diesel and electric locomotives.
🏭 Heavy Repair Workshop
Fully equipped for bogie overhaul, engine changeouts, and Periodic Overhaul (POH) of ALCO and EMD platform locomotives.
⛽ Fuel & Sand Depot
High-capacity HSD diesel storage with automated fueling gantries and sanding facility for locomotive preparation before duty.
⚙️ Dedicated EMD Section
Set up in 2009 alongside the WDG-4 induction, the EMD workshop has specialized tooling for GT46MAC platform maintenance.
📡 KAVACH / TCAS Integration
Ongoing fitment of Kavach anti-collision train protection system on eligible locomotives as part of Railway's safety modernisation programme.
🎓 Technical Training Centre
Continuous in-service training for loco pilots, technicians, and engineers on EMD, ALCO, and electric locomotive systems.
📋 Loco Management System
Computerised Loco Management System (LMS) for real-time tracking of loco status, maintenance schedules, and crew assignments.

History & Milestones

  • 1978
    Shed Commissioned — 8 June 1978
    Diesel Loco Shed, Sabarmati was established under the Western Railway's Ahmedabad Division with an initial holding capacity of 55 YDM-3 and YDM-5 metre-gauge locomotives.
  • 1998
    Full Loco Holding Capacity Reached
    The shed reached its designed holding capacity and became one of the largest MG loco sheds in Western India, supporting critical metre-gauge operations across Gujarat.
  • 2008
    Broad Gauge Transition — 50 WDG-4 Inducted
    In a landmark development, 50 WDG-4 broad-gauge EMD locomotives were added to the shed, heralding the transition to a high-horsepower, modern traction facility.
  • 2009
    First EMD Locomotive Arrives — WDG-4 No. 12224
    The first EMD locomotive, WDG-4 #12224, arrived in July 2009. The dedicated EMD workshop became fully operational. Serial production WDG-4s followed, building the fleet to 125+ units.
  • 2012
    WDG-4D Prototype Assigned to SBT
    The first WDG-4D prototype (plate WDG-4-668) was assigned to Sabarmati shed, making it one of the first sheds in India to operate the next-generation dual-cab freight locomotive.
  • 2014
    Serial Production WDG-4D Induction Begins
    Serial production WDG-4D locomotives (running numbers 703xx onwards) began arriving at sheds across India. Sabarmati received a significant allocation for freight corridor operations.
  • 2023–24
    International Export — Nine YDM-4 Locos to Togo
    Nine YDM-4 metre-gauge locomotives from Sabarmati shed were sold to Togorail SA and shipped to the Republic of Togo — a significant milestone for Indian Railways locomotive exports globally.
